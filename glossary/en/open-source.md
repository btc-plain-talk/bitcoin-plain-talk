# Open Source

**Category:** Community

## Plain English

Think of open source like a public recipe. Imagine a chef publishes their exact recipe online for everyone to see, copy, cook, and even improve. Anyone can take that recipe, tweak the spices, and share their own version  no permission needed, no secrets kept. Bitcoin’s software works the same way: its complete code is published publicly, so anyone in the world can inspect it, verify it does what it claims, copy it, or build something new from it. Nothing is hidden behind closed doors owned by one company.

## Analogy

Imagine someone in your neighborhood discovers a delicious way to make pilau . Instead of keeping the recipe secret, they write down all the ingredients and cooking steps and share them with everyone.

Now, you can cook the same pilau, check the ingredients, change the spices to suit your taste, or create your own version and share it with others. You don't need to ask the original cook for permission.

That's how open source works!

Bitcoin is similar. Its software code is publicly available, so people around the world can examine how it works, verify its rules, copy the code, and build new projects using it.

The key difference is Just as sharing a pilau recipe doesn't mean everyone can change the food already cooked in your kitchen, Bitcoin's open source code doesn't mean anyone can change Bitcoin's rules whenever they want. Changes to Bitcoin require others in the network to adopt them.

In simple terms: Open source means the recipe is public, but everyone still decides whether to use or accept changes to it. ₿


## In Context

*"Bitcoin is open source, so anyone can audit the code themselves instead of just trusting what a company tells them."*

**What this means:** This sentence captures the core trust model that makes Bitcoin different from traditional finance. With a bank or crypto company, you have to take their word for it trusting their claims about security, reserves, or how transactions are processed, with no way to verify independently. Bitcoin flips this , its entire source code is public, so instead of blind trust, you get verifiable proof. Any programmer, researcher, or curious learner can personally examine exactly how the system works, check that promised rules are actually followed, and confirm nothing shady is hidden inside. This openness is what lets thousands of independent people worldwide police the system together, making deception far harder than in a closed, company controlled system.

## Why It Matters

Open source matters because it replaces trust me with verify it yourself. With Bitcoin, you don’t have to believe a company’s promises about how the money works you, or anyone you trust, can read the actual code. This is why Bitcoin is called trustless ,not because no one trusts it, but because you don’t need to trust any single person or company. For someone new to Bitcoin, this matters because it’s the foundation of Bitcoin’s honesty  thousands of independent developers and researchers worldwide constantly review the code, making it extremely hard for anyone to sneak in fraud or hidden rules.
